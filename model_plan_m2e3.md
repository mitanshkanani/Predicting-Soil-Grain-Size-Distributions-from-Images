# Model 2 — Experiment 3 implementation plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: use superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking. Do not begin any step until the user has approved this document.

**Goal:** Test whether a frozen DINOv2 embedding adds *information* to the 12 hand-computed features when the two are concatenated into the same frozen ridge head — the only way Model 2's premise can still survive after E1 refuted replacement and E2 refuted rescue-by-magnification.

**Architecture:** One new variable: the composition of the feature matrix. 12 columns become 12 + 384 = 396. Everything else is inherited unchanged from Model 2 E1/E2 — tiles, aggregation, scaler, curve basis, ridge, monotone projection, CV families, alpha grid, metric. The gated arm is the fusion at crop 256, E1's frozen magnification; crops 128/64/32 are fused as sensitivity only and cannot fire the gate. Two new control arms exist to stop a lucky mean from being read as complementarity: a row-permuted embedding block (identical column statistics, correspondence to the soil destroyed) and a random-weights embedding block.

**Tech stack:** numpy / scipy / pandas / scikit-learn / matplotlib / Pillow. **No `torch`, no `timm`, no GPU, no Kaggle run required.** E3 consumes the tile embeddings E2 already computed, so every arm is a ridge fit on arrays that exist on disk. `backbones.py` is not imported and `crop_geometry.py` is imported read-only for the geometry table.

**Spec:** this file is the spec. The governing prior findings are `Model 2/Model 2 Experiment 1/instructions.txt` (replacement refuted, the R artefact, the 60.56 probe), `Model 2/Model 2 Experiment 2/instructions.txt` + `Model 2/Model 2 Experiment 2/kaggle run 1 files/Experiment2.txt` (magnification refuted at all four crops), and `Model 1/instructions.txt` (CAM+RES demoted to ordinal-only, the parsimony rule).

## 1. What E1 tested, and what it actually established

**Tested:** whether 384 frozen DINOv2 ViT-S/14 CLS embeddings per tile beat the 12 hand features, under the identical head and CV.

**Established — replacement is refuted:**

| arm | in-domain LOGO-CV | CAM+RES | Kaggle |
|---|---|---|---|
| `M1` 12 hand features | **43.02** | 54.98 | 61.24 (E3's submission) |
| `D` DINOv2 384 | 44.33 | 47.08 at oracle alpha | 60.56 (declared probe) |
| `R` random-init ViT 384 | **39.78** | 78.55 | never submitted |

Three findings, and only the first is the headline:

1. **D did not beat M1.** 44.33 against 43.02. The premise of Model 2 — that a pretrained vision transformer would carry grain-size information the hand features miss — is false as a replacement claim.
2. **R appeared to win, and that is an artefact, not a result.** R's apparent advantage came with the signature of in-domain overfit: it selected the grid-floor alpha (0.03), it had the worst cross-camera transfer of any arm (78.55), and its per-soil distance to the training distribution was 0.0000. R is the E1 trap and we recorded it as such.
3. **D is enormously more invariant than M1 and it bought nothing.** camera/soil distance ratio 0.209 against M1's 1.105, blur/soil 0.029 against 0.818 — roughly 5x more camera-stable and 28x more blur-stable — yet 1.3 EMD *worse* in-domain. **Robustness and informativeness are independent.** That is E1's durable finding, and it is the only reason E3 exists.

E1 also declared one confound in advance: the patch footprint. It recorded `PATCH_MM = 3.0752 mm`, which omits the 256 -> 224 resize.

## 2. What E2 tested, and why it is a refutation

**Tested:** whether E1's refutation survives correcting that confound and sweeping magnification fourfold. A centred window of the 256 px tile is cropped to c in {256, 128, 64, 32} and resized to 224, for both `D` and `R`, against the frozen `M1` anchor.

**Corrected:** true footprint at crop 256 is `14 * (256/224) / 4.552516 = 3.5145 mm`, 14.3% larger than E1 recorded.

**Refuted — magnification rescues nothing:**

| arm | in-domain | vs M1 43.02 | vs R same crop | camera/soil | CAM+RES |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D@256 | 44.33 | +1.31 | +4.55 | 0.209 | 54.01 |
| D@128 | 43.32 | +0.30 | +0.96 | 0.252 | 51.33 |
| D@64 | 43.83 | +0.81 | +3.14 | 0.295 | 53.51 |
| D@32 | 44.87 | +1.85 | +6.24 | 0.444 | 58.59 |

- **D lost to R at every one of the four crops.** The best case is +0.96 EMD at crop 128; no CI came close to excluding zero.
- **No monotone trend.** Spearman rho = -0.40, p = 0.60 for D, and the *opposite* sign for R. With four points there is no trend to find, and the pre-registered read was the trend, not the best-of-four.
- **P4 was the leak canary and it came back negative in the right direction.** The 256 -> 32 change was *not* concentrated in the patch-limited fines (fines -0.68, mid -2.88, coarse +2.13). A systematic gain in the fines would have meant the crop was smuggling label information; it did not.
- **The instrument was proven before any conclusion.** E2's crop-256 arm reproduced E1's M1 mean to 0.0017 EMD, and the control feature matrix matched E3's to 2.8e-14. **E2's machinery is valid; E2's hypothesis is dead.** Nothing below inherits any belief that magnification helped.

## 3. The question that survives both, and the exact hypothesis

E1 says: as a **replacement**, DINOv2 is not better.
E2 says: that is **not an artefact of scale** — eight times more magnification still loses.

Neither experiment asked the third question. E1's own third finding is the one that stays unexplained: D is ~5x and ~28x more invariant than M1 while being slightly less accurate. Invariance that costs nothing is not the same thing as information. The two families may therefore be **making different errors on different soils**, which is the classic condition under which combining them beats either — and it is the only mechanism left by which a frozen backbone could matter to this target at all.

> **H3 (the hypothesis E3 tests):** concatenating the 12 hand features with the 384-dim DINOv2 tile embedding yields a lower nested in-domain EMD than the 12 hand features alone, by more than the same concatenation with a label-blind row-permuted embedding block, at E1's frozen magnification.

**We do not assume H3 is true.** The pre-registered prediction P3 is that H3 is **false**. E3 exists to find out which, with controls that make a positive result attributable.

## 5. Arms — what each one exists to test, and why it is there

| # | arm | columns | exists to test | why it cannot be dropped |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `M1` | 12 | the external anchor; everything is measured against it | without a reproduction of 43.0217 there is no experiment at all |
| 2 | `D@256` | 384 | the embedding alone, re-derived from the harvested arrays | it is the provenance check on the cached embeddings *and* clause G3's comparator |
| 3 | `R@256` | 384 | the random-init twin alone, at the same magnification | `Model 2/instructions.txt:98` makes M1, R and D a **standing requirement for every Model 2 experiment**, and says the notebook must refuse to evaluate its own gate when any is missing. E3 therefore carries R as an arm, not just as the source of FR's block. It costs one nested score and the arrays are already required |
| 4 | **`FUSE@256`** | 396 | **H3 itself — the gated arm** | the experiment |
| 5 | `SHUF@256` | 396 | what 384 extra standardised columns cost or gain when they carry **no** information about the soil | a fusion win of unknown cause is not a result; this arm names the cause or kills the claim |
| 6 | `FR@256` | 396 | whether the effect, if any, is specific to *pretraining* | E1's lesson: R looked best in-domain. If FR ties FUSE, the story is "more columns", not "DINOv2" |
| 7-9 | `FUSE@128/64/32` | 396 | does fusion depend on magnification | reported only. E2's own trap was reading the best of four crops as a finding |

**Nine arms.** Adding `R@256` is what Model 2's capacity-control rule demands; it changes no clause, no threshold and no comparator in section 8, and it is recorded here before the run rather than after.

**`SHUF` construction, pinned before the run.** Take `SOIL[("dinov2@c256","real")]` and permute its **rows** (the 24 soil records) with `np.random.default_rng(20260933).permutation`, applied once to the whole table before any CV split. Every column keeps its exact mean, standard deviation and covariance structure; only the soil -> embedding correspondence is destroyed. The permutation is label-blind (it never reads `Y`), seed-pinned, and its permutation vector is printed in the record so the arm is reproducible by hand.

## 6. Validation strategy, and why it is stronger here than in E1/E2

1. **No mock mode.** This is the deliberate structural change from E1/E2. Their `BACKEND=mock` path let a crash survive to Kaggle because an arm list was empty locally (`ndarray.median()` in E1's cell N2) and whole sections never executed. E3's only input is the real harvested arrays; if any required file is missing the loader **raises with the exact missing filenames** rather than degrading. There is no branch that runs locally and a different branch remotely.
2. **Provenance assertions before conclusions.** All three Model 2 arms must reproduce E2's crop-256 numbers: `M1` 43.0217, `D@256` 44.3302, `R@256` 39.7769, tolerance 0.05 EMD each. Plus: the tile-path lists inside `.paths.npy` must equal the manifest order E2 used, and each array must be `(expected_rows, 384)`. Failure stops the notebook.
3. **Library drift measured, not assumed.** Local numpy 2.5.2 / sklearn 1.9.0 against Kaggle's numpy 2.0.2 / sklearn 1.6.1. Evidence it does not matter: E2's *local mock* run produced M1 = 43.02 against Kaggle's 43.0217. Task 4 re-checks this on the D arm, where the arrays came from Kaggle.
4. **Determinism.** Run twice, byte-compare every emitted file.
5. **Validity gate inside the gate.** If `SHUF@256` beats `M1`, the CV is rewarding capacity and E3's entire table is unreadable. That condition is checked and reported before the fusion clauses are interpreted.
6. **Integrity proviso.** If `FUSE@256`'s oracle alpha lands at 1e6 (or 0.03), the grid truncated the fit, the arm was handicapped, and the verdict is **INCONCLUSIVE** — explicitly *not* a refutation of H3.
7. **The submission path executes even when nothing is written** — the arm is fit, the frame validated, the eight gates run, no file emitted, all labelled as a plumbing check. E1's lesson, kept.

## 7. The metric that determines success

`nested_in_domain` = mean over the 24 CV soils of trapezoid EMD between the predicted and true CDF, where each soil's prediction comes from a model fit on the 15 folds not containing it, with alpha chosen by inner folds only. **Lower is better.** Reference points: no-image floor 82.62 (recomputed live), rank-3 representation ceiling 7.35, `M1` 43.02, `D@256` 44.33.

Success = the three clauses in section 8. Statistical difference alone is not success, and the interpretation table in section 11 says what each combination means.

## 8. The decision gate — fixed before the run, executed as code

```
G1  in_domain(FUSE@256) <  in_domain(M1)      AND paired soil-bootstrap CI of (M1 - FUSE@256)
                                              excludes zero with FUSE better   [n_boot 4000]
G2  in_domain(FUSE@256) <  in_domain(SHUF@256) AND paired CI of (SHUF - FUSE)  excludes zero
G3  in_domain(FUSE@256) <  in_domain(D@256)                                   [dominance, no CI]

PROVISO  0.03 < oracle_alpha(FUSE@256) < 1e6   else verdict = INCONCLUSIVE and the gate
                                              may not fire
VALID    in_domain(SHUF@256) > in_domain(M1)   else verdict = INSTRUMENT INVALID and the
                                              gate may not fire

submit = G1 AND G2 AND G3 AND PROVISO AND VALID
```

Numerical thresholds, written down now so nothing can be chosen after seeing a number:

| quantity | value | source |
|---|---|---|
| comparator `M1` | 43.02173087964769 | E2 Kaggle, must be re-derived here |
| comparator `D@256` | 44.330245615450906 | E2 Kaggle, must be re-derived here |
| CI method | soil-level paired bootstrap, 4000 resamples, percentile interval | E1/E2 unchanged |
| significance | interval excludes 0.0 | E1/E2 unchanged |
| reproduction tolerance | 0.05 EMD on each control arm | E2's cell H3 tolerance |
| practically-meaningful band | 15.0 EMD (`ORDINAL_FLOOR`) | E4's parsimony rule; smaller differences have not ordered the leaderboard reliably |
| grid-saturation bounds | alpha strictly inside [0.03, 1e6] | the frozen grid |

**On clause G3 being partly redundant, stated rather than hidden.** Given E2's measured values, G1 (`FUSE < 43.02`) already implies G3 (`FUSE < 44.33`). It is kept explicit for two reasons: the reproduction in H3 could move D@256, and the claim under test is "fusion beats **either family alone**", not "fusion beats the hand features". A gate clause that is implied by current data is still a clause about the assertion being made.

## 10. Compute: no GPU. Exactly which E2 artifacts are required

E3 does **not** need torch, timm, DINOv2 weights, internet, or a GPU. Every embedding E3 consumes was computed by E2's Kaggle run and written to `/kaggle/working/.cache`.

Row counts are **not hard-coded anywhere in E3.** Each array's first dimension must equal the tile list E2 built it from, recomputed live by replaying E2's own selection: `GOOD` for the aggregation cells. The arrays themselves are built over **`tiles`** (tile_size_px 256 and materialized: 1976 rows, 1541 train + 435 test) for the `real` view and over **`TRAIN_TILES`** (`tiles` where `split == "train"`: 1541 rows) for `res14`/`res16` -- measured from the local manifests, not copied from a log. The `.paths.npy` list is the authoritative comparison, so a wrong count is caught by set equality, not by a number I typed into a spec. Sizes below are approximate and only used to sanity-check a download.

| array | shape | float32 size |
|---|---|---|
| `..._real.npy` (crop 256/128/64/32) | (1976, 384) **float64** | 6.07 MB each |
| `..._res14.npy`, `..._res16.npy` | (1541, 384) **float64** | 4.73 MB each |
| each `.paths.npy` | 1976 or 1541 strings | 0.11-0.16 MB |

`backbones.embed_tiles` returns **float64** L2-normalised rows (`.astype(np.float64)` at the end), so the arrays are twice the size a float32 assumption predicts. Measured from same-shape local mock files, not estimated.

**Required — 15 arrays plus their 15 `.paths.npy` siblings = 30 files, 79.6 MB measured** (15.92 MB per backend-crop triple: 6.07 + 4.73 + 4.73 + paths). Naming pattern:

## 15. Assumptions

- E2's cached arrays are recoverable from Kaggle. **Unverified** — see section 10's three routes; Task 2 Step 1 fails loudly rather than assuming.
- A soil-level row permutation is a valid label-blind null for a 24-row table. It preserves column statistics exactly; it does not preserve any *relation* between columns of the block and the soil, which is the point.
- Column scaling of a 396-wide matrix behaves as it did for 384. E2 already standardised 384 columns per arm at n=24, so this is an extension, not a new claim; `grid_spread` and `parsimony_is_grid_max` are printed to catch it if wrong.
- Local library versions reproduce Kaggle's ridge arithmetic. **Measured** on the M1 arm (43.02 both ways); re-checked on the D arm in Task 5 Step 3.

## 16. Risks

| risk | mitigation already in the design |
|---|---|
