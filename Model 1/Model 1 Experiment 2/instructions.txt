MODEL 1 — EXPERIMENT 2 — VALIDATION-INSTRUMENT CALIBRATION
================================================================================

WHAT THIS EXPERIMENT REPRESENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Experiment 2 does not test a model. It tests the RULER.

Its single question is: which internal selection criterion predicts the external
Kaggle score? Its deliverable is a selection rule, not an architecture.

Everything about the model is held identical to Experiment 1 — the same rank-3 PCA
curve basis, the same closed-form ridge, the same clip/cummax/force-100 monotone
projection, the same tile->image->soil median aggregation, the same 16 curve-distance
CV families, the same 24/10 split, the same EMD implementation. The only things that
change are (a) how configurations are scored and (b) whether two defective columns are
in the feature matrix.

WHY IT EXISTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Experiment 1's protocol selected ridge alpha = 0.03 on the strength of an internal
LOGO-CV score of 35.98. On Kaggle that configuration scored 172.70, against 102.37 for
a submission that ignores the images entirely.

The internal criterion did not merely overstate the result. Reconnaissance for this
plan showed it ranks alpha in the OPPOSITE order to reality: CV improves monotonically
as alpha falls, while held-out-camera transfer degrades. Any experiment scored by that
criterion records noise, and a deep network fine-tuned against it would waste the GPU
budget. The instrument has to be repaired before it is used again.

This is also a declared, documented DEVIATION from the FIXED-protocol rule in
`Model 1/instructions.txt`. See that file's amendment note. The rule was written before
the rule was measured, and the measurement failed.

WHAT IS BEING COMPARED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Factor A — selection criterion:
  LOGO-CV     Leave-one-family-out over the 16 curve-distance families. E1's rule.
  CAM-HOLDOUT Fit on one training camera's view of a soil, predict the other
              training camera's view of that same soil, with the soil's LABEL held
              out of the fit. 21 soils are photographed by both Motorola Edge and
              Samsung A52. Both directions, averaged.
  RES-MATCH   As CAM-HOLDOUT, but the target is a SYNTHETIC camera built from the
              training tiles by applying the extra anti-alias low-pass that the test
              images received and the training images did not. This is the only
              candidate whose shift magnitude can match the real train->test gap.

Factor B — feature set:
  16          Experiment 1's matrix exactly.
  14          E1's matrix minus soil_fraction and crop_area_fraction.
              soil_fraction is constant to four decimals across all 24 training soils
              (std 0.00027) yet ranges to 0.829 on test, i.e. -63.9 SD. A zero-variance
              training column cannot be learned, so its coefficient is a noise
              amplifier that fires only out of domain. This is a DEFECT CORRECTION, not
              a research variable, and it is reported separately for that reason.

Swept, never hand-picked: alpha in {0.03, 0.1, 0.3, 1, 3, 10, 30, 100, 300, 1000}.

CIRCULARITY CONTROL — the most important rule in this folder
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Selecting alpha on a criterion and then reporting that same criterion as the result is
circular. Every number in the final comparison table must come from a NESTED protocol:
for each outer held-out family, alpha is chosen using only the inner folds, and the
outer family is scored once with the chosen alpha. We report the score of the
PROCEDURE, never the score of the best alpha. A comparison of criteria is a comparison
of procedures.

PRE-REGISTERED PREDICTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Written before the run. The run may not edit this list.

  P1  LOGO-CV and CAM-HOLDOUT disagree on the best alpha by >= 2 orders of magnitude.
  P2  Dropping the two defective columns improves CAM-HOLDOUT transfer.
  P3  RES-MATCH reproduces a downward shift in EVERY texture band, matching the
      train->test pattern (e4 -0.66, e8 -0.58, e16 -0.38 SD).
  P4  The E2 submission scores BELOW 102.37, i.e. beats ignoring the images.
  P5  E2's actual score is ~1.5x its CAM-HOLDOUT prediction, matching E1's factor.

WHAT WOULD JUSTIFY A DIFFERENT VERDICT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
If all three criteria rank alpha differently from the external evidence, the honest
conclusion is that no internal criterion is adequate for this problem and Model 1 must
be selected on camera-robustness priors (heavy regularisation, no colour features)
rather than on any measured score. That is an acceptable outcome for E2 and must be
written into Experiment2.txt as the result, not hidden.

LIMITS OF THIS EXPERIMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * n = 21 soils and 2 camera pairs. A 6-EMD difference is not evidence. Report fold
    spread and refuse to rank configurations whose intervals overlap.
  * Motorola<->Samsung differ by 0.81 mean |z| on the same soil; training->test differs
    by 2.63 SD on blue alone. CAM-HOLDOUT is a LOWER BOUND on the real gap. Measured
    bias on E1's configuration: predicted 116.8, actual 172.70, factor 1.48x.
  * The public leaderboard is a fixed 3 of 10 test soils. One submission is a weak
    measurement. E2 gets one, and we will not read more into it than it carries.
