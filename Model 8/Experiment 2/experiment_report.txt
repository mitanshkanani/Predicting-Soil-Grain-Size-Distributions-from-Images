7. THE ID CONTRACT - THE TRAP IN THIS EXPERIMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The competition's sample_id values are NOT the internal sample ids. Test soil
'Muenster_BS6_9,0-10m' is 'HPC_Muenster_BS6_9_0-10m' on the board, and the internal id
arrives mojibake-corrupted ('M?nster...') through the feature CSV's encoding. The join
therefore goes through manifest_samples.submission_id, never through string surgery.

Verified: the three files' sample_id lists are identical and in sample_submission.csv
order. A submission with plausible-looking rows attached to the wrong soils would have
been catastrophic and would have scored nothing like 62.5.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ESTABLISHED
  - E2-A (handcrafted 12 features, colour raw) scores 55.80591 - the best external
    result this project has ever produced, beating 60.56167 by 4.756 EMD.
  - H2 IS REFUTED, and monotonically so. The more an arm destroys colour, the worse it

    robustness direction is backwards on this dataset.
  - Our internal ruler anti-ranks these three arms perfectly (Spearman rho = -1.00,

  - The handcrafted texture+colour block BEATS the DINOv2 embeddings by 4.756 EMD
    externally. Feature-space gap runs the opposite way to what the 43-vs-60 framing

  - All three arms are contract-valid and mutually distinct (14-26 EMD apart).

NOT ESTABLISHED
  - That E2-A's margin is REAL as opposed to board noise. 4.756 EMD is 0.36 SD of the
    3-soil public null (SD 13.13). The private board carries 70% weight and is the
    number that counts. A new public best is NOT a secured improvement.
  - WHY raw colour wins. Three arms cannot separate "colour is causally useful" from
    "the 7 colour columns happen to act as a useful 7-dimensional regulariser that the
    5 texture features cannot supply". The texture-only arm has 5 features against 12,
    so E2-A vs E2-B confounds colour CONTENT with feature COUNT.
  - Any generalisation claim. n = 3 arms, 10 test soils, one protocol.

THE HONEST BOTTOM LINE, stated for the owner who asked whether we are good or bad:

  We are NOT bad. 55.80591 is the best score this project has ever posted, from the
  simplest model in it - no deep features, no pretraining, no gradient training, 24
  soils, closed-form ridge. The whole "transfer gap" framing was chasing a protocol
  artefact. The model was fine; the SELECTION RULE was broken.

  We were, however, selecting arms with a ruler that anti-ranks them. That is now
  measured, not suspected, and it is the single highest-value thing to fix next.

10. BUGS FOUND DURING THIS RUN

10. BUGS FOUND DURING THIS RUN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Recorded because each would have produced a wrong answer silently.

(a) Wrong unpacking order on ruler.design(). It returns (X, Y, fam_list, ids); the call
    site read `ids, Y, fams, _ = R.design()`, binding `ids` to the 24x12 design matrix.
    Indexing a frame with a 2-D array indexes rows AND columns, which surfaced as
    "Cannot index with multidimensional key". Several wrong hypotheses were chased
    before printing type(ids) and seeing float arrays. Fixed to `_, Y, fams, ids`.

(b) transfer_eval.emd_pair builds its abscissa by str()-ing sample_submission.csv's
    column names, which yields a 2-D array on this pandas version and raises
    "only 0-dimensional arrays can be converted to Python scalars". Worked around by
    computing DL locally in build_e2.py. THIS IS A LATENT BUG IN A SHARED Model 5 FILE.
    It was NOT modified - Models 1-7 are out of scope for Model 8. Flagged here for its
    owner.

(c) rank_normalise indexing resolved to the pandas Index method rather than the column
    "split" in one call site. Fixed to f["split"].

12. THE ALPHA PROBE - PREDICTION REFUTED, AND THE CONTRADICTION RESOLVED
================================================================================
Alpha is the ONLY tuned quantity in the entire model. The 12 features, the rank-3
curve basis, the ridge and the projection are all fixed. So alpha IS the hypothesis
space, and Experiment 2 measured that the internal ruler anti-ranks arms. Two probe
submissions were pre-registered (alpha_probe.py) at alpha = 0.3 and 30, bracketing the
incumbent alpha = 3, before any score was seen.

    alpha   internal    EXTERNAL
    -----   ---------   ---------
     0.3     43.9182     59.38746
     3.0     41.1475     55.80591    <- argmin BOTH internally and externally
    30.0     51.7009     71.10598

    Spearman rho (internal vs external) = +1.000
    Pearson  r                          = +1.000
    external spread 15.30 | internal spread 10.55

I PREDICTED THE OPPOSITE and the prediction was wrong. The pre-registered hypothesis
was that the external optimum might sit where the internal ruler cannot see it. It
does not - internal picked alpha = 3, the board agrees, and the correlation across the
curve is perfectly POSITIVE.

THE CONTRADICTION, AND WHY IT IS NOT A CONTRADICTION
  Experiment 2's arm ranking: internal Spearman = -1.000 (anti-ranks).
  The alpha curve:         internal Spearman = +1.000 (ranks correctly).

Both are true, n = 3 each, and neither is statistically significant on its own. But
together they LOCALISE the failure, which is the point:

  - Within a single head, varying a continuous regularisation parameter, the internal
    ruler orders things CORRECTLY and tracks the external curve's shape.
  - Across DIFFERENT FEATURE SETS (12 raw vs 12 rank-normalised vs 5), the internal
    ruler orders things EXACTLY BACKWARDS.

So the internal ruler is not broken. It is a reliable interpolator that is being
asked a question it cannot answer. What it measures is "how well does this head
interpolate 24 labelled soils". What Experiment 2 needed was "how well does this head
transfer to 10 out-of-hull soils". Those are different quantities, and NO amount of
alpha tuning, feature engineering or arm-shopping inside the first one moves the
second.

  This also retires the "sample noise" explanation (mechanism 3 in plan.md section 2)
  as the PRIMARY cause. Three arms with a perfect anti-rank and a perfect positive-rank
  under a deterministic metric is not noise - noise would not produce rho = -1.000.

13. WHAT ACTUALLY CHANGED THE EXTERNAL SCORE - THE CAMERA FINDING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The external scores move far more with ALPHA (15.30 EMD spread) than with the colour
treatment (11.46 EMD spread). That says regularisation strength is the dominant lever
available, and the optimum is already found.

But the reason the arms anti-rank is visible in data E2 never looked at. Two columns in
features_soil.csv are unused by the frozen 12, and they are the whole story:

    cameras          train: Motorola Edge;Samsung A52 21, Motorola Edge 1,
                           Motorola Edge 60 Fusion 1, Samsung A52 1
                    test:  iPhone 16  x7, iPhone 14 x3

    ZERO iPhone images in training. TEN in test. And n_images shifts -1.371 SD
    (train mean 5.29, test mean 3.50).

The train/test split is a CAMERA SPLIT, not a random split. Every "extrapolation" this
project has measured (B 3.33 SD beyond range, lum_p50 2.22 SD) is a phone-manufacturer
colour-rendering shift, not a soil-property shift.

That reframes everything in section 8's "we are NOT bad" conclusion. It is still true
that 55.80591 is a real new best from the simplest model in the repo. But it is
achieved DESPITE a total camera change, and the reason it survives is the feature
space is handcrafted texture, which is far more camera-invariant than raw RGB. The
rank-normalisation arm (E2-C) failed precisely BECAUSE it destroys the absolute colour
scale - the one thing that still helps identify soil when the sensor is different.

This is a testable prediction, and it is FALSIFIABLE: the frozen head should transfer
BETTER to iPhone images than to unseen Samsung/Motorola images, per-soil. It is also
cheap to check - the split of the training set by camera is already in the data.

14. RECOMMENDATION FOR EXPERIMENT 3
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Do NOT build another arm on the handcrafted features. The alpha curve says we are at
the regularisation optimum, and Experiment 2 says the arm ranking is not knowable
internally.

The camera finding is the live lead. Concrete Experiment 3, three pre-registered arms,
all decidable EXTERNALLY because the camera split makes the question unanswerable
internally:

  E3-A  E2-A unchanged, refit with a camera-invariant colour space (CIELAB / opponent
        colour, or hue-saturation only). The train/test gap is a rendering shift, so
        moving to a perceptual space is the mechanistically motivated correction.
  E3-B  E2-A plus the two unused texture columns (spec_centroid_cpm, dom_wavelength_mm).
        Cheap, and they are the only unused signal in the table.
  E3-C  E2-A on the 5 texture-core features with colour added back PER-CAMERA... no.
        Replaced by: E2-A with alpha chosen to be deliberately non-optimal (alpha = 10)
        as a positive control that the external curve is a real curve and not noise.

  Three arms, declared now, all submitted, no selection. Same discipline as E2.
