MODEL 2 — EXPERIMENT 1 — FROZEN DINOv2 vs HAND-BUILT FEATURES
================================================================================

WHAT THIS EXPERIMENT TESTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
One question: does a frozen pretrained vision backbone extract more grain-size signal
from the existing tiles than Model 1's 12 hand-built features, and is any gain due to
PRETRAINING rather than to having more dimensions?

Single variable: the per-tile feature vector. The head, the aggregation, the curve
representation, the metric, the CV families, the split and the preprocessing are all
frozen at Model 1 E3's values. See `Model 2/instructions.txt`.

THE THREE ARMS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  M1  Model 1 E3's 12 hand-built tile features. The control. Its in-domain LOGO-CV is
      re-derived here rather than quoted, so the three arms are measured by one identical
      pipeline. (The 41.15 and 46.94 figures in Model 1's records were produced with alpha
      chosen by CAM+RES, which is not how alpha is chosen in this notebook.)

  R   DINOv2 ViT-S/14 architecture with RANDOM, UNTRAINED weights. The capacity control.
      Same 384 dimensions, same receptive field, same tiles, same aggregation, same head.

  D   DINOv2 ViT-S/14 with pretrained weights, frozen. The claim under test.

      R is not optional. A win for D that does not also beat R is evidence that
      DIMENSIONALITY helps, not that pretraining helps, and must be reported that way.

FEATURE DEFINITION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  tiles      the existing 1,976 materialised 256 px tiles. No re-tiling, no new size.
  input      each tile resized 256 -> 224 with bilinear interpolation, ImageNet-standard
             normalisation (mean [0.485,0.456,0.406], std [0.229,0.224,0.225]).
  readout    the CLS token, 384-dimensional, L2-normalised.
             RECORDED AS A CHOICE: patch-token mean pooling is a different readout and is
             deliberately NOT taken here, so that a later change is visible rather than
             silent.
  frozen     no gradient is ever computed. model.eval() under torch.no_grad(). The
             backbone is not trained, not fine-tuned, and not adapted in any way.
  aggregation  two-stage median, tile -> image -> soil, identical to Model 1.
             Median is kept even though mean is more conventional for embeddings, because
             changing it would be a second variable. mean-vs-median over embeddings is a
             legitimate later experiment and is noted in section 14.

HEAD, AND THE ONE DECLARED ACCOMMODATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The head is Model 1's exactly: fold-local StandardScaler, rank-3 PCA curve basis fitted
on training rows only, closed-form multi-output ridge on curve coefficients, then the
clip/cummax/force-100 monotone projection.

No embedding-PCA or dimensionality-reduction step is added, even though 384 features on 24
training soils looks degenerate. Ridge solves p > n in closed form, and inserting a
reduction step would be a second change to the pipeline and would make the comparison to
Model 1 uninterpretable.

The one accommodation: **the alpha grid is extended upward to 1e6.** A grid topping out at
1000 would silently under-regularise a 384-dim arm, which is the exact failure that caused
Model 1's E1 collapse. The extended grid is applied to ALL THREE ARMS including the
control, so it is not a differential advantage to the new arms. This is declared as a
necessary consequence of the representation change, not an independent variable.

WHERE EACH ALPHA RULE APPLIES — same split as E3 and E4, stated because it is easy to
get wrong:
  * the NESTED procedure score (the primary result) chooses alpha by plain inner-fold
    argmin, exactly as E3's nested engine did. Applying parsimony inside the folds would
    make the reported score a policy rather than a measurement.
  * the FINAL FIT used for a submission uses the E4 parsimony rule: the LARGEST alpha
    within 15 EMD of the grid optimum. Where the whole sweep is inside 15 EMD the rule
    lands on the top of the grid, which the notebook prints as an explicit warning instead
    of hiding. The mock arm does exactly that here, and it is the designed signal that the
    arm carries no alpha-resolvable signal.

PRIMARY METRIC
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In-domain leave-one-family-out LOGO-CV, nested, reported as the score of the PROCEDURE and
never of the best alpha.

This is a deliberate exception to Model 1's ruling that LOGO-CV may not select. The
justification is in Model 2/instructions.txt: E1 proved LOGO-CV mis-ranks under CAMERA
SHIFT, which is not the question this experiment asks. In-domain error is the only
quantity that was constant across all four Model 1 experiments, and it is the one the
representation question lives in.

CAM+RES is computed for all three arms and REPORTED, with a header saying it is not used
to select. It also becomes new calibration data for the ruler's failure analysis.

THE DECISION GATE — FIXED BEFORE IMPLEMENTATION, EXECUTED AS CODE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  submit = in_domain(D) < in_domain(M1)
           AND in_domain(D) < in_domain(R)
           AND the paired soil-bootstrap CI of (D - R) excludes zero

  outcome                                        meaning                    action
  D beats control and beats random-init          pretraining extracts signal  submit
  D beats control but not random-init            it is CAPACITY             do not submit
  D does not beat control                        representation is not the  do not submit
                                                 bottleneck
  any arm missing                                gate cannot be evaluated   do not submit

The gate is evaluated in code and its inputs are printed. It may not be renegotiated after
the numbers are seen. The second and third rows are real possibilities and both are useful
answers; this experiment is designed to be refutable, not to be vindicated.

PHYSICAL-SCALE CAVEAT, STATED UP FRONT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Tiles are 256 px = 56.23 mm at the canonical 4.5525 px/mm. ViT-S/14 takes 224 px, so one
patch spans 3.075 mm of soil.

Ten of the 24 training soils have D50 below 0.11 mm (measured in cell B1; the plan said
eleven, and the notebook prints the measured counts rather than the planned ones). For
those, a patch is roughly 28 times the median grain: the backbone sees fines only as
texture, never as particles. This is the same resolution floor that ruled out particle
counting during preprocessing, and it applies to any patch-based transformer at this scale.
Cell B1 reports three nested counts: soils where one patch spans more than three median
grains (12 of 24), soils whose median grain is smaller than a whole patch (16 of 24), and
the sub-0.11 mm group (10 of 24).

The fix — cropping a smaller physical area and upsampling so patches land at
sub-millimetre size — is a DIFFERENT VARIABLE and belongs in a later experiment. E1
deliberately does not do it, so that a null result is interpretable: if D fails here it may
be scale rather than representation, and the next experiment separates them. E1 alone
cannot fully exclude that reading, and this is the experiment's main known weakness.

PRE-REGISTERED PREDICTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Written before implementation. The blind/confirmation label is per prediction and may not
be edited afterwards.

  P1  the mock backend's in-domain LOGO-CV is at or worse than the no-image floor 82.62
      [CONFIRMATION - a plumbing correctness check. If mock scores WELL, the aggregation or
      the fold-local fitting is leaking and no real result may be trusted until fixed.]

  P2  DINOv2 embeddings shift LESS between Motorola and Samsung than the colour block does
      (Model 1 measured colour at 1.54 z mean camera shift)
      [BLIND - and the important diagnostic: if learned features are NOT more
      camera-stable, Model 2 inherits Model 1's transfer wall in full and invariance work
      is mandatory regardless of which representation wins.]

  P3  the random-init arm R does NOT beat the Model 1 control in-domain
      [BLIND - the ablation the experiment rests on]

  P4  the pretrained arm D beats the Model 1 control in-domain
      [BLIND - the central claim]

  P5  D beats R by a paired margin whose soil-bootstrap CI excludes zero
      [BLIND - pretraining rather than capacity]

  P6  if the gate fires and a submission is made, it beats 61.24
      [BLIND - the only external test]

P4 and P5 must BOTH hold for the gate to fire.

LOCAL VERIFICATION IS PARTIAL, AND THAT IS ISOLATED RATHER THAN HIDDEN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The development machine has no torch, no torchvision and no timm. The backbone arms
therefore cannot be executed locally at all.

The response is structural, not a substitution:
  - every torch import is confined to ONE file, backbones.py, behind a three-function
    interface
  - a pure-numpy `mock` backend exercises the entire surrounding pipeline locally: tile
    loading, resize, batching, caching, aggregation, fold-local fitting, nested CV, the
    paired bootstrap, the gate, the submission gates and the record writer
  - check_backbones.py tests the interface contract without torch, and asserts that the
    torch branches FAIL LOUDLY with a message naming Kaggle rather than silently falling
    back to mock

Consequence: a mock run is a plumbing test and is NEVER a result. The notebook refuses to
write a submission when the mock backend is involved, and the record labels a mock run
explicitly as verification. The real comparison requires one Kaggle run with both backbone
arms present, and the gate will not evaluate without them.

Budget two or three Kaggle runs for plumbing. The backbone path is genuinely untested until
the first one completes.

RESULT - RUN 1 (2026-09-28, no submission) AND PROBE RUN 2 (same day)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Run 1, 39/39 cells, 0 errors, torch 2.10.0+cu128 / timm 1.0.26, backbone construction
{'img_size': 224, 'dynamic_img_size': True}, control reproduced E3 to 2.842e-14:

    nested in-domain LOGO-CV     M1 43.02     D 44.33     R 39.78
    gate: all three clauses FAILED -> no submission
    P3 REFUTED  P4 REFUTED  P5 REFUTED  P2 CONFIRMED

  The premise is REFUTED. A frozen pretrained ViT does not extract more grain-size
  signal from these tiles than Model 1's 12 hand-built features.

  R's 39.78 is the best in-domain number this project has ever produced and it is
  an artefact, not a finding: not significant (paired -3.24, CI [-12.53,+6.06]);
  optimum at the BOTTOM of the alpha grid, which is exactly what E1 did before
  scoring 172.70; worst arm under transfer (CAM+RES 78.55 vs D's 47.08); and its
  between-soil cosine distance is 0.0000 - a random ViT collapses its outputs, so
  the scaler amplifies noise and an unregularised ridge fits it. Best in-domain
  plus worst out-of-domain is overfitting.

  The real finding is that robustness and informativeness separated:

      camera shift z    camera/soil distance    blur/soil distance
      M1 colour 1.541   M1 1.105                M1 0.818
      D         0.620   D  0.209                D  0.029

  D is ~2.5x more camera-stable and ~28x more blur-stable than anything Model 1
  built, and no more accurate. That overturns the assumption behind Model 1's
  camera work: making a representation device-invariant does not, by itself, make
  it informative.

PROBE RESULT - P6 CONFIRMED, and it does not rescue the premise
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    Submission_Model2_E1.csv (arm D, alpha 1000, 384 features)  ->  60.56167
    best Model 1 so far: E3                                      ->  61.23560
    improvement                                                  ->   0.674 EMD (1.1%)

  The prediction was that D beats 61.24. It did, so P6 is CONFIRMED. But the margin
  is small and the honest reading is a near-tie, not a vindication:

  * the two submissions really are different models - mean absolute difference 2.46
    percentage points, max 13.10, mean curve-to-curve EMD 13.40, and not one of the
    10 rows agrees within 1 pp. So 60.56 is not E3 re-scored.
  * but 0.674 EMD on the fixed 3-soil public set is inside what three soils can
    move by chance. It is a hint, not a result.
  * what the pair does show, and this is the useful part, is a SMALLER TRANSFER
    PENALTY for D: M1 goes 43.02 in-domain -> 61.24 external (+18.2), D goes
    44.33 -> 60.56 (+16.2). D starts worse and finishes level, which is consistent
    with the mechanism table. Two points on a three-soil sample, though - it
    motivates a direction, it does not establish one.

  Therefore: Model 2 does not continue as a source of accuracy. The pretrained line
  earns attention only as a route to a smaller transfer penalty, and that is a
  different claim than the one this experiment was built to test.

  Run 2 reproduced run 1's entire primary result table and the E3 reproduction line
  byte-identically across two separate Kaggle sessions - the pipeline is stable.

RULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Never overwrite a previous experiment's results. Model 1's folders are read-only.
Do not introduce fine-tuning, attention pooling, new tile sizes, another backbone, another
regression head, or any preprocessing change in order to make something work. If one of
them appears necessary, stop and record why; it is a different experiment.

A LOCAL MOCK RUN WRITES ONLY INTO `local mock dry-run/`. It must never share a directory
with real results, because the filenames are identical. This is enforced in cell A1, not
left to discipline: a mock dry run on 2026-09-28 overwrote the downloaded Kaggle outputs.
The numbers survived (the executed notebook carries every table, see
`Kaggle run 1 - recovered transcript.md`) but the CSVs did not.

THE DECLARED PROBE - REGISTERED 2026-09-28, BEFORE ANY PROBE SCORE WAS SEEN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Run 1 answered the question it was built to answer, and the answer was no:

    in-domain nested LOGO-CV   M1 43.02   D 44.33   R 39.78
    gate: all three clauses FAILED -> no submission

**The premise is refuted and that verdict is not revisited.** The probe is a separate
decision about a different question.

What run 1 also found is a split nobody expected: D is the most device-stable representation
this project has ever measured - camera shift 0.620 z against the colour block's 1.541 z,
camera/soil distance ratio 0.209 against Model 1's 1.105, blur/soil 0.029 against 0.818 -
and it is no more accurate. Robustness and informativeness came apart.

So the open question is which of those two matters for the score, and **no internal metric
can answer it.** That is not a preference; it is E1's measured finding - leave-one-out CV
ranks ridge configurations in the wrong order for cross-camera transfer, rank correlation
-0.953 - and CAM+RES, the ruler built to fix that, is demoted to ordinal-only after it
ranked E4 above E2 and the leaderboard reversed them. The only instrument that measures
transfer is the leaderboard.

    probe = submit arm D once, labelled as a probe, to test transfer directly
    prediction, fixed here and not editable afterwards:
        D scores BELOW 61.24 on the public leaderboard

    beats 61.24  -> cross-camera transfer is dominated by device stability, not in-domain
                    fit. Remaining time goes to invariance.
    does not     -> Model 2 is closed on evidence. Remaining time goes to hardening E3's
                    61.24 configuration.

Cost: one submission slot. It is armed by `Config.probe_arm = "dinov2"` in cell A1 and set
to None to disarm. Cell L1 prints the gate verdict first and the probe second, and
`Experiment1.txt` sections 9, 9b and 10 keep them visibly distinct so no later reader can
mistake a probe score for evidence about the premise.

**What the probe is NOT:** it is not a gate pass, not an improvement claim, and not a
reason to keep the pretrained line if it loses. If D beats 61.24 the conclusion is "pursue
invariance", not "DINOv2 was right" - the in-domain table already settled that.
