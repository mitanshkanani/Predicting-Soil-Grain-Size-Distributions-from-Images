4. WHAT ACTUALLY MOVED IN ARM B -- the regularisation finding
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Arm A selects alpha ['0.1', '0.3', '1', '3'] across folds; arm B selects alpha ['0.03', '0.3'].
  This is a measured mechanism, not a hypothesis: image rows scatter around the
  soil medians, so the residual sum of squares is larger at any given alpha, and
  nested selection answers by shrinking the penalty. The 1/n_images weighting
  holds total loss mass at one per soil as designed (asserted at every fold), but
  it cannot hold the RESIDUAL scale fixed, so arm B is effectively a materially
  less-regularised model than the incumbent, not merely a better-estimated one.
  Why that matters here is written in the project's own record, not invented:
    M1 E1  internal 35.98 -> external 172.70  (least regularised, worst ever)
    M2 E3  internal 39.26 -> external 83.94   (random-init ViT, best in-domain)
  An arm whose internal score improves by becoming less regularised is the exact
  signature that preceded this project's two worst results. Arm B is nonetheless
  submitted, because the pre-registered design says the board decides and this
  ruler's ranking is not evidence. The pattern is stated, not hidden, and the
  final-submission rule in plan.md 6b is what governs.
